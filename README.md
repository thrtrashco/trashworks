# The Trash Co. Marketing

Build a modern, responsive marketing website for "the trash co." — a tech-driven circular economy and environmental compliance consultancy based in India.

## BUSINESS CONTEXT
the trash co. helps businesses (Producers, Importers, Brand Owners) stay compliant with India's Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulations and transition to circular economy practices. They also supply high-grade Post-Consumer Recycled (PCR) polymers.

Tagline: "We work around Transparency, Trust & Trash"
Mission line: "Enabling a Circular Economy" — pave the way for a sustainable future by building a "Tech-Enabled Circular Economy," unlocking revenue potential from clients' waste streams.

## DESIGN DIRECTION — MUST BE UNIQUE, NOT A GENERIC TEMPLATE
- Theme: clean eco/sustainability aesthetic — mix of dark charcoal/black sections (like the brochure) with white sections, accented by a fresh green (#4CAF50-ish) and an earthy palette. Avoid generic stock "green nonprofit" look — make it feel premium/tech-driven, not preachy-NGO.
- Strong typography-led hero (bold display type for "Trust / Transparency / Trash" style stacked headline), similar energy to Elima (elima.in), Recykal (recykal.com), and Plastics For Change (plasticsforchange.org) — but do NOT copy their layouts, create an original composition.
- Use modern micro-interactions and motion: animated counters, scroll-reveal sections, hover-morph service cards, marquee/infinite-scroll for client logos.
- Component/UI libraries to pull inspiration & components from: React Bits, DaisyUI, Aceternity UI, Magic UI — use their interactive components (animated cards, spotlight/gradient hover effects, bento grids, marquee, text reveal animations) to make the site feel distinctive and modern, not a bootstrap-y default.
- Fully responsive — mobile-first, test breakpoints for mobile/tablet/desktop.
- Use a bento-grid layout for the "Services" and "Grades & Applications" sections instead of plain cards.
- Sticky/glassmorphism navbar.
- Subtle circular-economy motif (arrows/recycling loop) worked into section dividers or background patterns, referencing the brochure's recycling-loop hero graphic.
